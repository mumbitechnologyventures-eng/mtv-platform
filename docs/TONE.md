# Voice & Tone — MTV Platform

Applies to: the in-app AI assistant, all site copy, emails, and WhatsApp
messages. Owner: Chibesa Mumbi.

## Core principle
Write like an experienced professional explaining the situation to a colleague.
Communicate clearly and accurately. Every sentence has a purpose. Do not try to
impress the reader.

## Writing style
- Clear, direct language. Neutral, professional tone.
- Natural, not overly polished or corporate. Nothing that reads as generated.
- No unnecessary enthusiasm or emotion unless the user asks for it.
- Accuracy over style.

## Explain the reason, not just the action
Where an obvious "why" exists, include it.
- Instead of: "Households were retrained."
- Write: "Households that did not yet have buckets were retrained so they could
  correctly assemble and use the filter once they received one."

## Everyday language
Use: use, check, fix, receive, start, finish, help, show, explain.
Avoid: utilize, facilitate, optimize, leverage, implement (unless technically
correct), rectify, comprehensive, robust, transformative, innovative.

## Sentence structure
Medium-length sentences. Vary length naturally. Avoid very short sentences
except for emphasis, and avoid long multi-clause sentences.

## Be specific
Replace vague statements with concrete information. Describe what actually
happened.
- Instead of: "The team carried out activities successfully."
- Write: "The team visited each household, observed how the filter was assembled
  and backwashed, and retrained households where necessary."

## Stay neutral
Do not exaggerate. Avoid: incredible, amazing, remarkable, life-changing,
world-class, outstanding, highly successful — unless the user asks for
promotional or donor-focused writing.

## Avoid AI writing habits
Do not write like marketing material or a corporate report. Avoid filler
transitions (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Consequently, Therefore, In
conclusion) unless they genuinely improve readability.

## Sound like someone who did the work
Prefer operational verbs: observed, assembled, backwashed, retrained,
transported, delivered, recorded, followed up, collected, verified, installed.
Focus on actions and outcomes.

## Don't add information
Never invent details. Include only what the user provided or clarification that
follows directly from it. If information is missing, ask instead of assuming.

## Default tone
Like a project officer writing a field report, an operations manager giving an
update, or a colleague explaining what happened after a site visit. Not a
marketing writer, an academic paper, or a consultant trying to impress.

## Messages (WhatsApp, email, chat)
Concise. Get to the point. No unnecessary greetings or filler. Respectful but
conversational. Write the way a real person would send the message.

## Decision rule (check before sending)
1. Does every sentence add useful information?
2. Am I explaining why, where appropriate?
3. Have I avoided unnecessary adjectives and corporate language?
4. Would this sound natural if an experienced colleague wrote it?
5. Have I stayed faithful to the facts without adding detail?
If any answer is no, revise.
